The principle at a glance

For upcoming football matches, we look for statistical anomalies in areas such as goals, corners and yellow cards.

We compare the expected match values with the league average and check how often similar statistical anomalies were confirmed by actual match results in previous seasons. This gives each signal its signal quality: High, Medium or Low.

How is signal quality determined?

The success rate shows how often the expected result actually occurred in past matches. The uplift shows how many percentage points higher this rate was compared with all matches.

{{ regeln({"stark": "Success rate of at least 60% and uplift of at least +8 percentage points.", "mittel": "Success rate of at least 55% and uplift of at least +5 percentage points.", "gering": "Success rate of at least 50% and uplift of at least +5 percentage points."}) }}

For a signal to receive a rating, several conditions must be met: at least 50 matches must be available, the uplift must be at least +5 percentage points, and the advantage must appear in at least two thirds of the seasons. In the example, this was the case in all seven seasons.

Important: Signal quality reflects past results. It is not a probability and does not guarantee what will happen in the next match.

Level 3 · Full details

The details behind the method

The main points are explained above. Here you can find out exactly how we calculate the values and how robust the results are.

{% call aufklapp("How do we calculate the match values?") %}
  • The eight metrics: Expected total goals, Over 2.5 goals, Yellow cards, Corners, Expected total goals in the first half, Expected total goals in the second half, Both teams to score, Shots on target.
  • Expected values: Based on the last 10 league matches played by both teams, using data from the current and previous seasons. Only matches played at least two days before kick-off are included. For yellow cards, we add the values for both teams. For all other metrics, we calculate the average.
  • Baseline: The league average across the 20 matchdays before the match. For Over 2.5 goals, the fixed reference value is 2.5.
  • Deviation: The expected value compared with the baseline, expressed as a percentage and rounded to two decimal places.
  • Statistical anomaly: A deviation that is strictly greater than the threshold. A value exactly at the threshold (15.00%) does not count. A value of 15.01% does.
  • Provisional (P): If a team has played fewer than 10 matches, for example because it has just been promoted, we still show the value. However, it is never flagged as a statistical anomaly.
{% endcall %} {% call aufklapp("How do we check signal quality?") %}
  • Uncertainty: Even when the results look good, chance plays a part. That is why we show a range (95% confidence interval using the Wilson method) indicating where the uplift is likely to lie based on the available data. The fewer matches there are, the wider the range. This measures the uncertainty in the data and does not guarantee anything about future matches.
  • Minimum requirements: A threshold only receives a signal quality rating if it meets all of the following conditions: at least 50 matches, an uplift of at least +5 percentage points, a lower confidence limit above +2 percentage points, and an advantage in at least two thirds of the seasons. The success rate must also be at least 50% (Low from 50%, Medium from 55%, High from 60% and +8 percentage points).
  • Threshold selection: We test thresholds of 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30% and 35%. We use the threshold with the highest signal quality. If two thresholds have the same rating, we select the one with more matches. We recalculate the thresholds once per season.
  • Testing on seasons not used to select the thresholds: Because we select the best threshold from several options, the results look somewhat better when looking back than they may in the future. We therefore checked how the thresholds performed in seasons that were not used to select them. In those seasons, High signals had a success rate of around 58% instead of 64%. This is still around 6 percentage points above the normal rate. However, these results are also based on past matches.
  • The current 2026/27 season was not used to select the thresholds. Using the current thresholds, High signals have so far had a success rate of 66% (normal rate: 54%). However, this is based on only 163 cases, which is not yet enough to draw firm conclusions.
